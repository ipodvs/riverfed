# How to use this manual

This is your **side-by-side script** for the 35-slide AI Builders Handbook
deck. One section per slide. Each section gives you:

- **What's on the screen** — so you can glance and know which slide
- **What to say** — the spoken script, 30–90 seconds per slide
- **The rhetorical hook** — a question or beat designed to land even
  with a silent audience. Don't expect answers; expect a half-second
  of head-tilt.
- **Bridge** — a one-liner that gets you cleanly to the next slide

## Runtime

- **Body:** ~35 minutes at a steady pace
- **+ Q&A:** add 10–15 minutes
- **Total:** ~45–50 minutes

## Tone

- Conversational, not lecturing. You're a builder talking to builders.
- Specific over general. Names, numbers, products.
- Confident without being preachy. *"Stop me if this surprises you"* > *"This is groundbreaking."*
- Pause after rhetorical questions. Three full seconds. Let it land.

## Audience-handling tips

- **Don't actually expect hand raises.** The questions are designed to
  work whether or not anyone answers. If someone does, riff on it
  briefly and return to the script.
- **If energy dips around slide 16–18**, drop in the parenthetical
  *"Yes, I'm aware this is a lot. Hang with me — module 6 has the
  punchline."*
- **If someone interrupts with a long question**, answer in one
  sentence and offer: *"Let me park that for the Q&A — happy to go
  deep there."*

# Opening warmup (before slide 1)

Before you click into the deck, get the room awake. Try one of these:

> *"Quick show of hands — and I won't make anyone defend the answer —
> how many AI training sessions have you sat through this year?"*

(Pause. Look around. Nod knowingly regardless of the count.)

> *"OK. Last one of those today. Hopefully this one's a bit different."*

Or — if it's a smaller room and you want to set stakes:

> *"Three of you are going to leave here and try one of the things I'm
> about to describe by the end of this week. The other people will not.
> I'm not going to ask you which group you're in. But the difference
> between the two is the only thing that matters."*

Click into slide 1.

# Anticipated audience questions

Pre-baked answers for the ones that come up every time. Don't memorise
them — just have the shape ready.

## "Which harness do you personally use?"

> *"Claude Code, terminal-first. But I run Codex when I need GUI
> ergonomics or when the work is document-heavy. Honestly, the choice
> is overrated. I'd recommend whichever your workflow fits — the
> investment is in the four files, not the harness."*

## "How do you stop the agent going off the rails?"

> *"Three layers. First, restrict the tool surface via allowedTools
> — give the agent only what it needs. Second, permission modes —
> accept-edits auto-accepts file changes but still asks for shell
> commands. Third, the context — a good agents.md with explicit
> 'never do this' rules prevents 90% of drift."*

## "Doesn't this all break when GPT-6 comes out?"

> *"The opposite, actually. The four files — agents.md, memory.md,
> MCPs, skills — are portable across models. If anything, smarter
> models make rich context files more powerful, not less. The
> investment compounds. The harness might get faster. Your files
> still work."*

## "What about the privacy / security implications?"

> *"Real concern. Three rules. First, secrets stay out of agent
> context — use environment isolation. Second, every MCP gets the
> least-privilege scope you can give it — read-only when possible.
> Third, agent-owned accounts separate from your own. The wiki has
> a 'security as a layered problem' concept page if you want detail."*

## "I'm a marketer — do I really need to know any code?"

> *"You need to be comfortable in a terminal. That's about it. The
> code is mostly markdown — agents.md, memory.md, skill files. The
> shift in marketing is towards 'vibe marketing' — orchestrating
> agents rather than writing code. Look at the AI Marketing Masterclass
> source in the wiki for the exact stack."*

## "How much does this actually cost?"

> *"Depends entirely on usage. On the Claude.ai Max subscription,
> a daily-use operator burns most of their quota — call it
> $100–200/month. On the Anthropic API with prompt caching, a
> well-engineered workflow runs $200–500/month for serious volume.
> The Tiny model — $40K/month — replaces 8–10 humans. The unit
> economics are the point."*

## "What's the one thing you wish you'd known earlier?"

> *"That memory.md is the thing. Not the prompts. Not the model. Not
> the harness. The append-only log of corrections is what makes the
> agent get better over weeks instead of starting from zero every
> Monday."*
